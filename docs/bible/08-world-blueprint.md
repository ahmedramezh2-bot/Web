# HEBRA MASTER PRODUCTION BIBLE

## THE WORLD BLUEPRINT

*Documentation only. Pre-production. Nothing in this document is implemented by its own existence.*

====================================================

PREAMBLE

The world must be designed before anything inside it is built.

Not sketched — designed, the way a city is designed before a single building is placed: with a hierarchy of importance, a logic of movement, and a reason for every location to exist where it exists. This document is that design.

HEBRA is not a website. HEBRA is not a level. HEBRA is not an open-world game. HEBRA is an explorable cinematic civilization — a place that behaves as though it had a history before the visitor arrived and will have a future after they leave, and that the visitor is permitted to move through, not around.

*This document defines Level 2 (Physical Locations) of the Canonical Naming Architecture (docs/bible/26). The specific canonical location names and their assigned Environmental Dialects live in that document's §3; this Blueprint defines the design principles those locations follow, not their final name list.*

Where this document is silent, the Creative Director Protocol (Appendix G) governs. This document is read together with the Camera Bible (world navigation, §10) and the Lighting/Material Bibles (per-environment dialects) — this Blueprint defines *where things are and why*; those documents define *how they are seen and made*.

====================================================

## 1. WORLD PHILOSOPHY, SPATIAL IDENTITY, HIERARCHY, NAVIGATION PHILOSOPHY, ARCHITECTURAL PHILOSOPHY

**World Philosophy.** The world exists to be inhabited by a story, not to be a backdrop for one. Every location's first design question is "what happens here, emotionally," never "what can we build here, visually."

**Spatial Identity.** HEBRA's space is not Euclidean-neutral. Distance, scale, and direction all carry meaning (cross-reference Camera Bible §9's narrative-distance-not-literal-distance principle) — two locations that are emotionally close are built to feel close regardless of literal unit distance between them.

**World Hierarchy.** Not every location carries equal weight. Landmarks (§5) anchor the world; Transition Spaces (§4) connect it; Hidden Zones (§4) reward it. A Blueprint that treats every location as equally important produces a world with no rhythm, the spatial equivalent of a camera with no rhythm (cross-reference Camera Bible §7).

**Navigation Philosophy.** The visitor paces, never steers (cross-reference Camera Bible §10 verbatim) — this Blueprint's entire layout is authored assuming a camera-paced journey along a designed path, never a free-roam layout retrofitted with a path afterward.

**Architectural Philosophy.** Architecture in HEBRA is never decorative massing. Every structure's form follows from what it represents, what it protects, or what it channels (cross-reference Lighting Bible §1's "architecture produces light" principle) — form follows meaning before it follows function, and follows function before it follows aesthetics.

====================================================

## 2. NAVIGATION

The complete set of directions the world must support meaningfully, cross-referenced directly against Camera Bible §10:

**Forward / Backward** — the primary axis of narrative progress; backward exists only as an authored beat (a Pull, Camera Bible §8), never as literal free reverse.

**Left / Right** — the world threads between landmarks laterally; no long stretch of the primary path is a dead-center rail.

**Vertical** — Rise and Dive (Camera Bible §8) require genuine vertical variation built into the world itself — floating structures, descending chambers, rising thresholds — not merely a flat world with an elevated camera.

**Diagonal** — a natural consequence of combining the above; the Blueprint never authors a separate "diagonal path" as its own system.

**Depth** — near/mid/far layering exists at every point along the primary path, supporting the Camera Bible's mandatory frame-layering rule (Camera Bible §20).

**Circular / Spiral** — landmarks are built to be approached, orbited, and departed from convincingly in the round (cross-reference Camera Bible §8's Orbit and Spiral) — no landmark may be a "flat" backdrop that only reads correctly from one angle.

**Bridge Traversal / Portal Traversal** — distinct connective tissue types: bridges are read-through connections between two visible places; portals are opaque connections whose destination is deliberately unknown until arrival (cross-reference Camera Bible §8, Portal Entry/Exit).

**Floating Paths / Hidden Paths** — reserved specifically for the Discipline Zones and Hidden Zones (§4) and the Hidden Discovery System (§9) — never used on the primary chapter path, where such ambiguity would undermine the visitor's trust in the authored route (cross-reference Camera Bible §19's trust principle).

====================================================

## 3. WORLD LAYOUT — LANDMARKS

**Landmarks** anchor chapters and are the primary hero geometry of a given region.

**Monuments** are the largest, most narratively weighted landmarks — one or more per chapter, visible from unusually long distances to serve as wayfinding anchors even before the camera formally arrives at them.

**Floating Structures** communicate the civilization's mastery over its own physical rules — reserved for later chapters (post-Fragments) where the story has earned a departure from strictly grounded architecture.

**Ancient Bridges** are Bridge Traversal's physical form (§2) — always visibly load-bearing, always leading somewhere the visitor can partially see before crossing.

**Energy Rivers** are the world's visible circulatory system — cross-referenced against Lighting Bible §5 (Energy sources) and FX Bible §6 (Energy Philosophy) — they connect landmarks the way a nervous system connects organs, never as decorative flowing light with no destination.

**Memory Wells** are small, discoverable landmarks tied to lore rather than scale — cross-reference §9's Hidden Discovery System.

**Observation Platforms** are built specifically to justify Observing-state camera holds (Camera Bible §6) — a platform exists where the story wants the visitor to stop, not the other way around.

**Living Architecture** responds, subtly, to presence or story progress (cross-reference Lighting Bible §2's Living Light and Material Bible §7's Material Evolution) — reserved for the most emotionally significant structures.

**Impossible Geometry** is rationed as tightly as the Camera Bible rations Impossible Light and Spiral moves — reserved for singular, high-impact narrative moments, never a recurring background texture.

====================================================

## 4. DEFINE — THE MAIN PATH, DISCIPLINE ZONES, HIDDEN ZONES, SECRET CHAMBERS, TRANSITION SPACES, MEMORY SPACES, ORIGIN SPACES

*Terminology note: per the Canonical Naming Architecture (docs/bible/26), "World" is reserved exclusively for the single, singular HEBRA world — it is never used as a taxonomy term for a sub-region. What this section previously called "Main World," "Secondary Worlds," and "Hidden Worlds" are renamed below accordingly; nothing about their underlying design changes.*

**The Main Path** *(formerly "Main World")* — the primary, always-traversed chapter path (Void → Monument → Fragments → Origin → Awakening → Threshold, per the Story Registry). Every visitor experiences the Main Path in full.

**Discipline Zones** *(formerly "Secondary Worlds")* — the post-Origin functional layer's six named Physical Locations (The Creation Zone, The Identity Zone, The Architecture Zone, The Technology Zone, The Imagination Zone, The Legacy Zone, per the Canonical Naming Architecture §3) — reached via Service Transition (Camera Bible §8), optional in depth of exploration but structurally part of the civilization, not a bolted-on menu.

**Hidden Zones** *(formerly "Hidden Worlds")* — spaces that exist outside the Main Path's authored route entirely, discoverable only through the Hidden Discovery System (§9) — never required for narrative comprehension, always rewarding for visitors who find them.

**Secret Chambers** — smaller-scale than Hidden Zones, nested inside existing Landmarks (§3) rather than freestanding — a chamber inside a Monument, not a separate destination.

**Transition Spaces** — corridors, thresholds, and connective architecture whose sole purpose is to carry the visitor between two Landmarks or two Worlds with their own distinct lighting register (cross-reference Camera Bible §8, §10's "never visually identical tunnel segments" rule).

**Memory Spaces** — tied narratively and visually to Memory Light and Memory Stone (Lighting Bible §2, Material Bible §3) — softer, quieter regions dedicated to lore rather than spectacle.

**Origin Spaces** — the singular Origin Core and its immediate surroundings, held to a different design standard than every other space in the Blueprint (§8's per-location "purpose" field is at its most concentrated here) because the entire world's narrative logic radiates outward from this one location.

====================================================

## 5. EVERY LOCATION MUST DEFINE

No location enters this Blueprint without a written answer for each of the following:

- **Purpose** — why this location exists at all.
- **Story Role** — what narrative function it serves (cross-reference the Story Registry's chapter structure).
- **Emotional Role** — which target emotion (cross-reference Camera Bible §4's dialect table) this location is built to produce.
- **Lighting Identity** — cross-referenced directly against Lighting Bible §8.
- **Material Identity** — cross-referenced directly against Material Bible §8.
- **Camera Behaviour** — which Camera Language moves (Camera Bible §8) this location is specifically built to support or require.
- **Sound Behaviour** — owned by the Audio Production Bible §2, but logged here as a required field so no location is designed in audio isolation.
- **Navigation Behaviour** — which of §2's navigation types this location primarily uses.

A location missing any of these eight fields is not yet a documented location and may not be treated as production-ready.

====================================================

## 6. SCALE

**Human Scale** — used sparingly, almost exclusively in intimate Memory Spaces and Secret Chambers, where the visitor's implied body-scale is meant to feel present and small in a comprehensible way.

**Architectural Scale** — the default register for Discipline Zones and Transition Spaces — large enough to feel civilizational, small enough to remain legible without a Monument's full grandeur.

**Monumental Scale** — reserved for Landmarks (§3) specifically — scale here is itself a narrative device, not merely a size choice.

**Cosmic Scale** — reserved for Void and any moment the Blueprint deliberately wants the visitor to feel the civilization as small against something larger than itself.

**Transition Rules** — a location never jumps scale registers abruptly; every scale change across the journey is mediated by a Transition Space (§4) explicitly built to let the visitor's sense of size adjust gradually, mirroring the Camera Bible's requirement that every state change be eased (Camera Bible §9, §14).

====================================================

## 7. LANDMARK PHILOSOPHY

**Every landmark must be recognizable.** A landmark's silhouette must be identifiable from a Camera Bible §20 screenshot test — shown a single frame with no context, a visitor familiar with HEBRA should be able to name which landmark they're looking at.

**No duplicate silhouettes.** Two Landmarks with a similar outline is a Blueprint failure, regardless of how each performs individually — landmarks exist partly as wayfinding, and wayfinding depends on distinctiveness.

**Every landmark should be memorable from every direction.** Per §2's circular/spiral navigation requirement, no landmark may have a "correct" viewing angle and a series of unconsidered backs and sides — every approach direction receives the same design attention.

====================================================

## 8. ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING

**Nothing exists only for decoration.** Every object, surface, and structure placed in the world traces to a purpose per §5's required fields — a Blueprint element that cannot answer "why is this here" does not belong in the world.

**Everything tells history.** Consistent with the Material Bible's aging philosophy (Material Bible §2) and the Lighting Bible's Ancient/Memory Light identities (Lighting Bible §2), the world's own layout should read as something that has existed for a long time, not something constructed for the visitor's arrival.

**Everything has purpose.** Purpose does not require literal function — atmosphere is a valid purpose, so long as it is a deliberate one rather than an unconsidered default.

**Everything rewards observation.** A visitor who stops and looks closely at any given location should find more than a visitor who passes through quickly — this is the spatial-design expression of the Camera Bible's hesitation-as-meaning principle (Camera Bible §3).

====================================================

## 9. HIDDEN DISCOVERY SYSTEM

**Secret routes** — alternate Bridge or Portal Traversal paths (§2) leading to Hidden Zones or Secret Chambers (§4), never signposted, always plausible in hindsight once found.

**Hidden chambers** — cross-reference §4's Secret Chambers; discoverable through close observation rather than through any UI hint system (cross-reference UI Integration Bible §4's "no traditional navigation" principle).

**Observation rewards** — content, detail, or lore exposed only to visitors who linger (cross-reference §8's rewards-observation principle) rather than to visitors who simply progress through the Main Path quickly.

**Repeat visitor rewards** — content that changes or newly reveals itself on a return visit, cross-referenced against the Technical Addendum's compressed Overture for returning visitors — the world itself, not just the pacing, is permitted to acknowledge a repeat visit.

**Environmental puzzles** — spatial arrangements that require the visitor to notice a relationship between two or more locations before a Hidden World or Secret Chamber becomes accessible — always solvable through observation alone, never through externally-provided hints.

**Invisible details** — the smallest tier of reward: a Material Bible §5 micro-engraving, a Lighting Bible §5 Symbol light, present whether or not any visitor ever finds it — existing for the world's own internal consistency as much as for discovery.

====================================================

## 10. PERFORMANCE PHILOSOPHY

Bound by Appendix F without exception.

**Streaming** — the world loads progressively along the authored path, prioritizing atmosphere over detail exactly as Appendix F specifies, never blocking on a fully-loaded scene before allowing the journey to begin.

**Chunk Loading** — the world is partitioned by chapter and by World type (§4), loading and unloading in step with the Camera Bible's Chapter Transition and World Transition beats (Camera Bible §8), so a chunk boundary and a narrative boundary are always the same boundary.

**LOD** — Landmarks (§3) carry the most detail tiers, given their role as long-distance wayfinding anchors (§7); Transition Spaces (§4) carry the fewest, since they are, by design, traveled quickly.

**Occlusion** — Secret Chambers and Hidden Zones (§4), being nested or off the Main Path, are aggressively occluded until proximity or discovery makes them relevant, keeping their cost near-zero for the majority of visitors who never find them.

**GPU / Memory Budget** — allocated first to whichever chapter or World is currently active, per Appendix F's draw-call and geometry tiers, with Landmarks drawing the largest individual share within that allocation.

## 11. RESEARCH

Per Appendix E's standing protocol, applied here specifically:

**Architecture and Ancient Civilizations** — studied for how real historic sites communicate age, purpose, and hierarchy through layout alone, directly informing §1 and §8.

**Modern Museums** — studied for how curated physical space guides a visitor's attention and pacing without explicit signage, informing §9's discovery philosophy and the "no traditional navigation" principle shared with UI Integration Bible §4.

**AAA Level Design** — studied for landmark-based wayfinding, critical-path versus optional-content structuring, and scale transition technique, directly informing §6 and §7.

**Environmental Storytelling** — studied as its own discipline (object placement communicating narrative without text) for §8's core principle.

**Three.js, R3F, official documentation** — studied for streaming/LOD/occlusion technique only, per Appendix D and Appendix E, never for ready-made world templates.

Extract principles. Never imitate.

====================================================

## 12. WORLD OATH

I existed before the visitor arrived, and my layout proves it.

Every location in me has a reason. Nothing is here only because a space needed filling.

I do not repeat my own silhouette. Every landmark I hold can be told apart from every other, from any direction.

I reward attention. A visitor who lingers finds more than a visitor who hurries, and both experiences remain complete in their own right.

I hide things honestly — my secrets are never signposted, and they are never unfair to find.

I change scale gradually, never abruptly, and I never ask the visitor to steer — I only ask them to walk.

I hold my performance budget without losing my geography — a cheaper version of me is still recognizably the same world.

Every future hand that adds to HEBRA's world inherits this oath before it inherits any level file.

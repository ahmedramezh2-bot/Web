# HEBRA MASTER PRODUCTION BIBLE

## THE LIGHTING BIBLE

*Documentation only. Pre-production. Nothing in this document is implemented by its own existence.*

====================================================

PREAMBLE

Light is the first thing HEBRA gives a visitor, and the last thing it takes away.

Before there is a monument to see, there is light deciding whether the monument may be seen at all. Before there is a story to understand, there is light deciding what mood that understanding will arrive in.

This document is written the way a cinematographer thinks, not the way a renderer thinks. It answers *why* light exists in every space before any future document answers *how* it is technically produced.

Where this document is silent, the Creative Director Protocol (Appendix G) governs. Where this document conflicts with the Camera Bible on light-as-composition matters, the two are read together — the Camera Bible owns how light is *framed*, this document owns what the light *is* and *means*.

====================================================

## 1. LIGHTING PHILOSOPHY

**Why light exists.** Light exists to make meaning visible, not to make geometry visible. A monument lit only so it can be seen has been lit incorrectly — it must be lit so that what it *represents* can be felt. Illumination is a side effect of storytelling, never the goal.

**Why darkness exists.** Darkness is not the absence of a lighting budget. Darkness is the visual expression of the unknown, the unresolved, the not-yet-earned. HEBRA's darkness is authored with the same intent as its light — every unlit region exists because something in the story has not yet given the visitor permission to see it clearly.

**The emotional purpose of illumination.** Every lighting decision is judged first against the emotion it is meant to produce (§2's identity list, §4's chapter table) and only second against whether it reads clearly. A technically correct light that produces the wrong feeling has failed this Bible even if it passes every other measure.

**The relationship between architecture and light.** HEBRA's architecture is never lit from outside as a generic subject. Light is treated as something the architecture itself *produces, channels, or withholds* — monuments have light the way living things have a pulse, not the way objects have a spotlight pointed at them. Where a structure's own material or narrative role would not plausibly emit or channel light, ambient and environmental sources (§5) carry the scene instead — light is never draped over a surface for convenience.

====================================================

## 2. LIGHT IDENTITY

Every light source in HEBRA belongs to one of eight identities. A light with no identity is an unauthored light and does not belong in the world.

- **Natural Light** — the sky, distant stars, ambient environmental glow. Untouched by the civilization; existed before HEBRA and will exist after it. Communicates: *scale, indifference, time.*
- **Artificial Light** — light plausibly produced by the civilization's own systems (channeled energy, worked material). Never electrical in appearance — HEBRA has no light bulbs, no visible fixtures. Communicates: *intention, craft, presence of a maker.*
- **Ancient Light** — light with visible age: dimmer than it once was, uneven, slightly imperfect. Found in the oldest structures. Communicates: *history, endurance, things that have survived.*
- **Energy Light** — light that is visibly alive: pulses, flows, has direction and apparent purpose (see Appendix D — never generic "RGB energy," always narratively motivated). Communicates: *life force, the civilization's own vitality.*
- **Living Light** — light that responds, however subtly, to presence — brightening, softening, shifting almost imperceptibly as the visitor nears. Reserved for the most emotionally significant objects. Communicates: *the world is aware of you.*
- **Impossible Light** — light that could not exist under real physics (light without a plausible source, light that behaves against gravity or expectation) — used exceedingly rarely, only at moments the story explicitly calls for the uncanny or the sacred. Communicates: *this is beyond the civilization's ordinary rules.*
- **Memory Light** — light tied to a specific recollection or lore beat — often desaturated, softer, slightly non-present, as if seen through recollection rather than in the moment. Communicates: *the past, briefly surfacing.*
- **Origin Light** — the singular light identity reserved for the Origin chapter and the Origin Core environment (§8). The most saturated, most intentional, most "real" light in the entire experience — everything else in the lighting language is, in some sense, an echo of this one source. Communicates: *the beginning of everything else.*

No scene mixes more than three identities without deliberate narrative justification — an overcrowded light identity palette reads as indecisive, not rich.

====================================================

## 3. DARKNESS

Darkness is never empty. Every dark region in HEBRA is authored with as much intent as every lit one.

**Depth.** Darkness gives light somewhere to end, which is what makes light legible as light. A frame with no darkness has no shape — value only exists in contrast.

**Mystery.** What is withheld in darkness is a narrative promise — that region will matter later, or it would not have been left unlit. Darkness is HEBRA's version of the Camera Bible's Withholding (Camera Bible §3) expressed through illumination instead of framing.

**Scale.** A monument's true size is often best communicated by how much of it recedes into darkness — the visitor's imagination completes what the light doesn't confirm, and imagination always outscales rendering.

**Silence.** Darkness is illumination's version of a held camera frame (Camera Bible §7) — a deliberately unlit beat is scored into the experience's rhythm exactly like a lit one, with its own planned duration.

**Expectation.** Darkness ahead of the visitor's path (rather than around them) creates forward-pull — the same psychological mechanism as a Camera Reveal (Camera Bible §8), produced by lighting rather than camera motion.

**Hope.** In the Threshold chapter specifically, darkness is used as the visible *absence* that the chapter's rising light is meant to resolve — hope is felt most sharply as a contrast with what came immediately before it.

**How darkness behaves:** it is never pure black (true zero-light reads as a rendering bug, not a mood) — HEBRA's darkness always carries a faint ambient floor, sourced from Natural Light (§2), so the world remains readable as a physical place even at its dimmest.

====================================================

## 4. COLOR TEMPERATURE

Color temperature is HEBRA's primary emotional dial, read in cooperation with the Color Bible but authored here as a lighting behavior first.

- **Warm** — used at moments of arrival, understanding, and intimacy (Origin, Threshold's resolution). Warmth is earned, never a default.
- **Cold** — the default register for Void and most of Traveling state — isolation and scale read more truthfully in cool light. Cold is not "unfriendly"; it is vast.
- **Neutral** — used in functional, post-Origin discipline spaces where clarity matters more than mood — never fully neutral (a trace of the chapter's dominant temperature always survives), but closer to true white than any other register.
- **Ancient** — a desaturated, slightly warm-leaning neutral specific to Ancient Light sources (§2) — the color equivalent of patina.
- **Unknown** — reserved for Impossible Light — a temperature that reads as slightly wrong to the eye without being nameable as a specific hue shift, used to unsettle deliberately and briefly.
- **Living** — Energy Light's temperature, distinct per Discipline Zone (a different living hue signature per The Creation/Identity/Architecture/Technology/Imagination/Legacy Zone, logged for Color Bible cross-reference — see Color Bible §7).

**Emotional transitions.** Temperature shifts happen the same way camera dialects shift (Camera Bible §4) — gradually, across a chapter's duration, never as an abrupt cut between two fixed values. A visitor should be able to feel a chapter's emotional temperature *changing* even if they can't say exactly when it changed.

====================================================

## 5. LIGHT SOURCES

Every light in the world traces to one of these origins. An unattributable light source is an authoring failure.

**Sky** — the primary ambient source for outdoor and open registers; carries Natural Light identity by default.

**Stars** — distant, near-negligible direct illumination but meaningful *presence* — their visibility is itself a lighting decision about how much of the sky's depth the visitor is allowed to perceive at any given chapter.

**Energy** — the civilization's living current (§2, Energy Light) — traced visually along the same channels FX Bible §6 defines for energy behavior, so light and effect read as one coherent system rather than two overlapping ones.

**Artifacts** — small, personal, often Memory Light or Ancient Light sources — objects that carry their own illumination as a signal of individual significance within the larger world.

**Architecture** — structures that channel rather than generate light — carved channels, translucent sections, apertures that shape ambient or sky light into directed beams.

**Monuments** — the largest-scale light sources, often Origin- or Energy-adjacent, whose illumination is proportional to their narrative weight, not their physical size.

**Symbols** — small, precise, often the *only* light source in an otherwise dark micro-scene — used exactly like a spotlight on a held object in film, to direct absolute attention.

**Crystal** — refracts and disperses rather than emits — crystal light sources are defined by what they *do to* other light more than by what they produce themselves (cross-reference §11, Reflection).

**Unknown Matter** — the rare Impossible Light carrier (§2) — logged, not detailed further here, because its specific behavior is deliberately kept undefined until a specific narrative moment calls for it.

**Fog** — does not emit but *reveals* — fog is lit by everything around it and, in turn, makes every other source in the scene visible as a volume rather than a point (cross-reference §6).

**Reflection** — secondary, bounced light — never modeled as a separate "fake" source, but as the visible consequence of Material Bible surfaces interacting with primary sources above (cross-reference §11 and Material Bible §6).

====================================================

## 6. VOLUMETRIC PHILOSOPHY

Volumetrics exist to make light itself visible as a physical thing moving through space, not as a decorative haze layered over a scene.

**Fog, Mist, Dust** are three distinct densities and behaviors, not synonyms: Fog is the heaviest, used to obscure and control reveal pacing at world-scale (cross-reference Camera Bible §21, Environmental Awareness). Mist is lighter, used at human/architectural scale to soften edges without obscuring readability. Dust is the finest and most granular, used as near-field atmosphere (cross-reference Camera Bible §20, foreground layering) and to make individual light shafts visible without a full volumetric fog pass.

**Atmosphere** is the ambient carrier for all of the above — every environment has a baseline atmospheric density appropriate to its narrative register (Void: sparse and cold; Crystal Garden interiors: denser and warmer).

**Energy**, when volumetric, behaves as a directed flow rather than a diffuse cloud — it moves with implied purpose, per §2's Energy Light identity.

**Light Shafts** are reserved for moments of arrival or revelation — they are never ambient wallpaper; a light shaft's presence is a compositional promise that something along its length matters (cross-reference Camera Bible §20's leading-line exploitation).

**Scattering** governs how directional a given volumetric pass reads — tighter scattering for dramatic shafts, broader scattering for ambient haze — chosen per beat, never left at a single global default.

**Density and Depth** are the two parameters volumetrics use to communicate scale: increasing density with distance is HEBRA's primary tool for making vast spaces read as vast (cross-reference §12, Atmospheric Perspective) without requiring any actual increase in world size.

====================================================

## 7. SHADOW LANGUAGE

Shadows are not the inverse of light — they are their own authored vocabulary.

**Soft Shadows** — the default register for most of the world; large, diffuse sources produce shadow edges that feel organic rather than computed. Hard-edged shadows are the exception, not the baseline.

**Hard Shadows** — reserved for moments needing graphic clarity or tension — a single strong source (often Symbols or Origin Light, §5) producing a crisp, legible shadow shape used as a compositional device in its own right.

**Layered Shadows** — multiple overlapping shadow sources (a primary source plus bounced/secondary light) that together produce depth-through-shadow rather than depth-through-light — used in dense environments (Crystal Garden) where shadow complexity communicates intricacy.

**Moving Shadows** — shadows that shift as Energy Light or environmental elements move — a way of making a static piece of architecture feel inhabited by implying motion nearby even when the camera itself is in Observing state.

**Invisible Shadows** — deliberately near-absent shadow in specific reverent beats (Awakening dialect, cross-reference Camera Bible §4) — the visual equivalent of held breath; used exceedingly rarely because its power depends on scarcity.

**Psychological Shadows** — shadow shapes that are legible as something *other* than their casting geometry — an intentional, rare device (a shadow that reads briefly as a figure, a symbol, a face) used only where the story explicitly calls for unease or revelation, never as an incidental accident of geometry.

====================================================

## 8. ENVIRONMENTAL LIGHTING DIALECTS

*Terminology note: what this section calls "Dialects" are Level 3 Environmental Dialects per the Canonical Naming Architecture (docs/bible/26) — reusable lighting languages, never locations in their own right. See that document §3 for which Level 2 Physical Location uses which Dialect.*

Each Dialect owns a lighting register — same eight identities (§2), same shadow language (§7), different emphasis — the lighting equivalent of the Camera Bible's per-chapter dialects (Camera Bible §4).

- **The Attentive Dialect** *(formerly "Library")* — Ancient Light dominant, soft shadows, warm-neutral temperature; illumination pools locally around individual objects rather than filling the whole space, rewarding close attention.
- **The Reverent Dialect** *(formerly "Temple")* — Origin- and Energy-Light dominant, hard shadows used sparingly at its most sacred point, the most vertically dramatic light shafts (§6) in the world.
- **The Active Dialect** *(formerly "Workshop")* — Artificial and Energy Light, warmer and more active than The Attentive Dialect, shadows that move (§7) to suggest ongoing, unseen labor.
- **The Vast Dialect** *(formerly "Observatory")* — Natural Light dominant almost to the exclusion of all else, the coldest temperature register outside Void itself, minimal shadow complexity so the sky remains the undisputed subject.
- **The Hushed Dialect** *(formerly "Archive")* — Memory Light dominant, desaturated, deliberately underlit relative to every other interior — the visitor works slightly to see, which is the point.
- **The Genesis Dialect** *(formerly "Origin Core" as an environment name — the Physical Location keeps the name "The Origin Core"; the Dialect used there is now named separately)* — the single Dialect permitted full-intensity Origin Light — the brightest, most saturated, most narratively "true" light anywhere in HEBRA.
- **The Generative Dialect** *(formerly "Creation Chamber")* — Energy and Living Light dominant, the most volumetrically active Dialect (§6), shadows that layer and shift continuously.
- **The Recollective Dialect** *(formerly "Memory Hall")* — Memory and Ancient Light in combination, the softest shadow language in the world, temperature drifting slowly rather than holding steady, echoing §4's un-anchored, past-tense emotional register.

**Each Dialect must have its own lighting register while remaining part of one civilization** — achieved by keeping the underlying eight-identity system (§2) and shadow vocabulary (§7) constant everywhere; only the *mix and intensity* changes Dialect to Dialect, never the grammar itself. A single Physical Location may draw on more than one Dialect where its World Blueprint entry calls for it.

====================================================

## 9. TIME

Light evolves. A static lighting setup, however well-composed, eventually reads as a still image rather than a living world.

- **Breathing** — the lighting equivalent of Camera Bible §9/§23's camera breathing: extremely slow, low-amplitude intensity and color-temperature drift, always present, never perceived as flicker.
- **Expansion** — light that grows in reach or intensity at a chapter's rising narrative moments (paired with Camera Bible's Rise, §8).
- **Relaxation** — the inverse: light that recedes and softens as a beat resolves, paired with Camera Bible's Pull or Settle (§8, §23).
- **Pulse** — rhythmic, deliberate intensity variation tied specifically to Energy Light and Living Light sources (§2) — never applied to Natural or Ancient Light, which do not plausibly pulse.
- **Awakening** — the specific, once-per-visit lighting arc that accompanies the Dormant→Awakening camera state (Camera Bible §6) — the visitor's very first perceptible light, authored with the same care as a film's opening shot.
- **Rest** — the lighting state paired with the camera's Resting state (Camera Bible §6, Final Scene) — light recedes to its lowest sustained intensity of the entire journey, without ever reaching true black (§3).
- **Silence** — a held, unchanging lighting frame, authored with intentional duration exactly like a camera hold (Camera Bible §7) — light's own version of a scored pause.

====================================================

## 10. REFLECTION

**Reflection philosophy.** Nothing in HEBRA reflects perfectly. A perfect mirror reads as a rendering demo, not a material with history (cross-reference Appendix D). Every reflective surface carries some imperfection — age, dust, micro-texture — that keeps its reflections honest to the object's story.

- **Water** — the softest, most diffuse reflections in the world, reserved for the rare beats where water appears at all; used to slow the visitor's read of a scene, never for spectacle.
- **Crystal** — refractive more than reflective — light passes through and disperses (cross-reference §5) rather than bouncing cleanly back.
- **Obsidian** — the sharpest, darkest reflections in HEBRA — used deliberately for its ability to hold a scene's brightest highlights against near-total blackness.
- **Metal** — reserved for Liquid Metal and Ancient Alloy material families (cross-reference Material Bible §3) — reflections here carry the most legible environmental detail of any surface type, used at moments the story wants the world to visibly repeat itself.
- **Stone** — the most common, most muted reflective behavior in the world — barely-there sheen rather than a clear image, keeping most architecture grounded and matte.
- **Unknown Matter** — reflection behavior deliberately left undefined here, same reasoning as §5 — authored only when a specific narrative moment requires it.

====================================================

## 11. ATMOSPHERIC PERSPECTIVE

**Distance, Fog, Scale, Depth, Haze, Contrast, Hierarchy** — the seven parameters that together make far things read as far without moving the camera an inch farther than it already is.

As distance from the camera increases, contrast compresses, color desaturates slightly toward the environment's ambient temperature (§4), and edges soften — the same principle atmospheric perspective has always described in painting and cinematography, applied here as a live, continuous function of camera-to-subject distance rather than a fixed background matte painting.

**Hierarchy** is the payoff: because near, mid, and far elements are lit with deliberately different contrast and clarity, the eye is guided toward the correct subject in a busy frame without any explicit vignette or spotlight being required — atmospheric perspective is, in this sense, a lighting-driven composition tool that cooperates directly with the Camera Bible's foreground/midground/background layering rule (Camera Bible §20).

Haze density scales with the active Dialect's baseline volumetric density (§6) — the Void chapter's atmospheric perspective is the most extreme and coldest in the experience; interior Discipline Zones use the gentlest, since their scale is architectural rather than cosmic.

====================================================

## 12. INTERACTION WITH CAMERA

**Composition.** Light is one of the primary tools (alongside Camera Bible §20) that builds a frame's foreground/midground/background hierarchy — the camera and the lighting system are, in effect, co-authors of every composed shot, never independent layers stacked on top of each other after the fact.

**Emotion.** Every chapter dialect in the Camera Bible (§4) has a corresponding lighting dialect in this document (§8's environmental table, §4's temperature table) — the two must be authored together, checked against each other, and never allowed to drift out of sync (a "curious" camera dialect paired with a "resolved" lighting temperature is a continuity failure).

**Curiosity.** Light withholds exactly as the camera withholds (§3, Darkness; Camera Bible §3, Withholding) — partial illumination of a subject is lighting's version of a Camera Reveal's partial-information requirement (Camera Bible §8, §14).

**Navigation.** Per §6 and §11, light and atmosphere are a legitimate wayfinding tool — a brighter register ahead of the visitor's path reads as invitation, without ever requiring a literal marker, breadcrumb trail, or UI arrow (cross-reference UI Integration Bible §4's "no traditional navigation" principle).

====================================================

## 13. INTERACTION WITH MATERIALS

Light should reveal materials. Materials should shape light. Neither dominates.

A lighting setup authored with no knowledge of what it will fall on is incomplete — every Environmental Lighting Dialect (§8) assumes specific material families (Material Bible §3) are present, and light intensity/color/shadow softness are chosen in service of what those materials need to read correctly (obsidian needs strong highlight control; stone needs soft, broad illumination to avoid looking flat).

Symmetrically, no material is authored assuming a single lighting condition — every material must read coherently across at least Natural, Artificial, and Energy Light (§2), because the same object is very likely to be seen under more than one of them across a single journey.

This mutual dependency means the Lighting Bible and the Material Bible must be read and revised together whenever either changes — logged here as a standing cross-document dependency enforced by the Constitution Review Protocol §4's Consistency Validation.

====================================================

## 14. PERFORMANCE PHILOSOPHY

Bound by Appendix F (AAA Performance Budget) without exception; this section is the lighting-specific reading of that budget.

**Desktop.** Up to Appendix F's ceiling of six real-time shadow casters — allocated by narrative importance, not by scene population; a beat's single most important light-casting object always wins the budget before any secondary or decorative source. Volumetric passes (§6) are reviewed per beat for their actual contribution to composition — a volumetric layer that isn't doing legible compositional work per §12 is cut before it ships, regardless of how it looks in isolation.

**Mobile.** Appendix F's ceiling of two real-time shadow casters means most of the Environmental Lighting table (§8) must rely on baked or hybrid lighting for anything beyond the single most important live source in frame. Volumetric density (§6) is the first parameter reduced under Tier B/C, never the light's color temperature or identity — a scene may look flatter on a constrained device, but it must never look like the *wrong* scene.

**Adaptive Quality.** What may degrade: shadow resolution, volumetric sample count, number of simultaneous dynamic sources, reflection sample count (§10). What must never degrade: light identity (§2) assignment, color temperature (§4), the presence versus absence of a light source that carries narrative meaning (Symbols, Origin Light) — a budget-constrained tier may render a light more cheaply, but it may never simply remove a light that the story depends on.

## 15. RESEARCH

Research is mandatory before any future implementation, per Appendix E's standing protocol, applied here specifically:

**Film lighting** — study how Roger Deakins, Greig Fraser, and Hoyte van Hoytema use practical-feeling motivated light and confident darkness to build scale and restraint without over-lighting a frame. Extract: the discipline of *not* lighting something just because it's there.

**Museum lighting** — study how museums light single objects to communicate value and fragility through restraint and precise, narrow illumination — directly informs §5's Symbols behavior and §8's Library/Archive dialects.

**Architectural lighting** — study James Turrell's treatment of light as sculptural material in its own right, and Tadao Ando's use of light as the primary architectural element rather than a finishing touch — both inform §1's core claim that light is never draped over architecture but produced *by* it.

**AAA games** — study cinematic-lighting-driven titles for how real-time engines fake film-grade motivated lighting within a frame budget, directly informing §14.

**Physically Based Rendering** — study PBR theory for how light and material interact honestly, informing §13's mutual-dependency principle, even though this document authors intent rather than shader math.

**Three.js, R3F, WebGPU research, official documentation** — studied for technique only, per Appendix E and Appendix D — never for ready-made lighting rigs to be shipped unchanged.

Extract principles. Never imitate.

====================================================

## 16. FORBIDDEN LIGHTING

Per Appendix D's standing authority, the following are permanently forbidden regardless of how they look in isolation:

- **Random glow** — any glow without a traced Light Source (§5) is forbidden.
- **Overexposed bloom** — bloom exists to sell a source's intensity, never to add generic sparkle.
- **Fake god rays** — light shafts (§6) that don't originate from an actual authored source and volumetric context are forbidden.
- **RGB lighting** — no light identity (§2) is ever chosen for decorative color variety; every hue traces to §4's temperature logic and a chapter's or room's authored register.
- **Neon everywhere** — Energy Light (§2) is rationed and purposeful, never an ambient decorative wash.
- **Meaningless emissive materials** — an emissive surface with no Light Source attribution (§5) and no narrative role is forbidden.
- **Uniform illumination** — a scene lit evenly, with no atmospheric perspective (§11), no shadow language (§7), and no hierarchy is a lighting failure regardless of technical correctness.

====================================================

## 17. FUTURE EXPANSION

New Story Chapters, Physical Locations, or Discipline Zones introduced after this document's authorship inherit the eight Light Identities (§2), the shadow vocabulary (§7), and the atmospheric perspective system (§11) as their base grammar — a new Physical Location draws on one or more existing Environmental Dialects (per §8's pattern, cross-reference docs/bible/26) rather than inventing a new lighting language from nothing.

Any proposed ninth Light Identity, or any proposed new Shadow Language category, is treated with the same weight as a Camera Philosophy amendment (Camera Bible §26) — it changes the grammar every future room draws from, and requires full review rather than being folded in silently by a single implementation.

VR/AR and any future rendering pipeline change (WebGPU) inherit this document's *intent* unconditionally; only its *technical expression* (§14, §15) is expected to change with the underlying platform.

## 18. LIGHTING OATH

I exist to make meaning visible, not geometry.

I never illuminate something the story hasn't earned the right to show clearly.

My darkness is never empty — it is always a promise of something not yet given.

I trace to a source. I am never merely present because a scene looked flat without me.

I evolve. I breathe. I am never a static setup mistaken for a finished shot.

I cooperate with the camera and the material beneath me — I do not work alone, and I do not compete with either.

I hold to my performance budget without abandoning my identity — a cheaper version of me is still recognizably me.

I am never neon for its own sake, never bloom for its own sake, never glow without a reason. Every future hand that lights a beat of HEBRA inherits this oath before it inherits any renderer setting.

## 19. LIGHTING REVIEW CHECKLIST

- [ ] Does this light trace to one of the eight identities in §2, with no ambiguity?
- [ ] Does its color temperature (§4) match its chapter's or room's authored emotional register?
- [ ] Is its darkness authored with intent (§3), not left as an unconsidered gap?
- [ ] Does it use the correct shadow language (§7) for its narrative weight?
- [ ] Does it cooperate with the camera's current dialect (§12) rather than contradicting it?
- [ ] Does it respect the material it's falling on, per §13?
- [ ] Does it hold Appendix F's frame and shadow-caster budgets, measured per §14, not assumed?
- [ ] Does it evolve over time (§9) rather than sitting as a static setup?
- [ ] Has it been checked individually against every bullet in §16?

## 20. PRODUCTION APPROVAL CHECKLIST

- [ ] Philosophy: the light's *reason* for existing is written down before its *look* is.
- [ ] Identity: assigned, singular, traceable.
- [ ] Environment: matches its room's dialect in §8 or explicitly, deliberately breaks it for a logged narrative reason.
- [ ] Research: principle extracted and logged per §15, never a copied reference.
- [ ] Performance: reviewed against Appendix F on both desktop and mobile targets.
- [ ] Accessibility: does not introduce a photosensitivity risk (cross-reference Camera Bible §25).
- [ ] Final Test (Appendix E): with all reference material hidden, does this light still feel unmistakably HEBRA's?

If every box is checked, lighting work may proceed to Creative, Engineering, Performance, Accessibility, and Identity Review per the Production Roadmap. If any box is unchecked, the work returns to this document, not to the renderer.

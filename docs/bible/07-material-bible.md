# HEBRA MASTER PRODUCTION BIBLE

## THE MATERIAL BIBLE

*Documentation only. Pre-production. Nothing in this document is implemented by its own existence.*

====================================================

PREAMBLE

Every surface in HEBRA has lived a life before the visitor touches it with their eyes.

A material is not a texture applied to geometry. A material is the physical memory of everything that object has been through — how it was made, how long it has existed, what has touched it, what light has done to it over time. This document defines that memory for every material family in the civilization.

Where this document is silent, the Creative Director Protocol (Appendix G) governs. Where this document conflicts with the Lighting Bible on how a surface responds to light, the two are read together — the Lighting Bible owns the light, this document owns what the light lands on.

====================================================

## 1. MATERIAL PHILOSOPHY

Materials are identity. A visitor who has never read a word of HEBRA's lore should still be able to sense, from surface alone, which objects are ancient, which are alive, which are sacred, and which are merely structural.

**Materials tell history.** Every material family's finish is a record of time passing — nothing in HEBRA is manufactured-clean by default. Newness, where it exists at all, is reserved for the rare object the story specifically needs to feel untouched.

**Materials carry memory.** A material's imperfections (§6) are not randomized noise — each one, in principle, traces to something that happened to that object. This is an authoring discipline, not a literal requirement that every scratch have a written backstory, but the visible language must always be *consistent with* a material having a history, never with a material having been generated.

**Every surface has meaning.** A material family is never chosen for an object because it "looks good here." It is chosen because it is the correct material for what that object represents, at that point in the story, in that environment.

====================================================

## 2. MATERIAL IDENTITY, HIERARCHY, AGING, EVOLUTION, INTERACTION

**Material Identity.** Every material family (§3) has a fixed core identity — its Visual Identity, Physical Behaviour, and History (§4) — that does not change from scene to scene. What changes is state (§6, §7), never identity itself. Obsidian is always obsidian; what varies is how much light, age, and energy that particular obsidian surface is currently showing.

**Material Hierarchy.** Not every material carries equal narrative weight. Hero materials (used on monuments and named artifacts) receive the most micro-detail authorship (§6) and the tightest Appendix F texture budget allocation. Environmental and background materials (§8) are simpler, more repeated, and read at a distance rather than up close — hierarchy in material authorship mirrors hierarchy in the Camera Bible's framing-as-emphasis principle (Camera Bible §3).

**Material Aging.** Aging is authored per family (§3), not applied as a single global "old" filter. Stone ages by erosion and softening of edges. Metal ages by patina and micro-pitting. Crystal ages by internal clouding rather than surface wear. Aging must always be legible as *that specific material's* way of aging, never a generic weathering pass.

**Material Evolution.** Beyond static aging, some materials are permitted to change *during* the visit itself (§7) — most notably Living Glass and Energy Crystal, whose evolution is tied to narrative proximity and progress rather than to elapsed real time.

**Material Interaction.** Materials are never authored in isolation from their neighbors. Where two material families meet (a Crystal inlay in a Stone monument), the seam itself is a designed detail — materials in HEBRA visibly *know* what they're next to.

====================================================

## 3. MATERIAL FAMILIES

- **Black Obsidian** — the world's darkest, sharpest material; used for structural and sacred surfaces that need to hold the world's brightest highlights against near-total blackness (cross-reference Lighting Bible §10).
- **White Stone** — the world's primary neutral architectural material; soft-edged, quietly aged, the canvas most other materials are set against.
- **Unknown Crystal** — refractive, internally complex, the material family most associated with Impossible Light (Lighting Bible §2) and mystery generally.
- **Liquid Metal** — a material that behaves as though perpetually on the edge of motion; the most reflective family in the world (cross-reference Lighting Bible §10), reserved for objects tied to transformation or creation.
- **Ancient Alloy** — a worked, historic metal family, less liquid and more structural than Liquid Metal, carrying visible age (§6) as its dominant characteristic.
- **Living Glass** — a family capable of authored Material Evolution (§2, §7); translucent, subtly responsive, used where the story wants an object to feel aware.
- **Energy Crystal** — the primary carrier of Energy Light (Lighting Bible §2) in solid form; internally lit rather than surface-lit.
- **Transparent Minerals** — softer, calmer cousins of Unknown Crystal, used for smaller-scale and more intimate objects (Artifacts, per Asset Production Bible §2).
- **Impossible Matter** — deliberately undefined in detail here, mirroring the Lighting Bible's treatment of Unknown Matter (Lighting Bible §5) — authored only when a specific narrative beat requires it.
- **Architectural Stone** — the load-bearing, large-scale cousin of White Stone, used specifically for monumental structure rather than surface finishing.
- **Sacred Stone** — visually related to Architectural Stone but reserved exclusively for objects and spaces tied to the Origin and Awakening chapters — its distinction from ordinary Architectural Stone must always be legible even at a glance.
- **Memory Stone** — desaturated, softer-edged, paired narratively with Memory Light (Lighting Bible §2) and the Archive/Memory Hall environments.

No material family may be introduced for a single object and never reused — every family exists because it will recur across the world, giving the visitor a growing, learnable material vocabulary the same way the Camera Language (Camera Bible §8) is a learnable vocabulary of movement.

====================================================

## 4. EVERY MATERIAL MUST DEFINE

Before any material family may be considered documented, it must have a written answer for each of the following:

- **History** — how this material came to exist in HEBRA's world, in outline.
- **Purpose** — what kind of object this material is used for and why.
- **Visual Identity** — the immediately recognizable, describable-in-one-sentence look of the material.
- **Physical Behaviour** — how it would behave under real or invented physics (rigid, liquid-adjacent, brittle, self-healing).
- **Reflection Behaviour** — cross-referenced directly against Lighting Bible §10's per-material reflection entries.
- **Surface Behaviour** — roughness, texture grain, consistency across the object versus localized variation.
- **Edge Behaviour** — how the material's edges read: sharp, worn, chipped, softened, luminous.
- **Weathering** — the family-specific aging language established in §2.
- **Imperfections** — the specific micro-detail vocabulary (§6) applicable to this family.
- **Interaction with Light** — cross-referenced against Lighting Bible §13.
- **Interaction with Darkness** — how the material reads when underlit; some families (Energy Crystal, Living Glass) remain partially self-evident in darkness, others (White Stone) recede almost completely.
- **Interaction with Energy** — whether and how Energy Light (Lighting Bible §2) visibly moves across or through this material.

A material family missing any one of these twelve fields is not yet a documented material and may not be treated as production-ready.

====================================================

## 5. MICRO DETAILS

Micro detail is what separates a material that has *lived* from a material that was merely rendered.

- **Scratches** — directional, consistent with plausible use or handling, never randomly scattered.
- **Dust** — settles logically with gravity and airflow (cross-reference Lighting Bible §6, Atmosphere) rather than uniformly coating a surface.
- **Micro Cracks** — a sign of age or stress specific to brittle families (Stone, Crystal); never applied to Liquid Metal or Living Glass, which age differently.
- **Micro Engravings** — small, deliberate marks that reward close observation (cross-reference World Blueprint §9's Hidden Discovery System) — never decorative noise.
- **Tiny Symbols** — the smallest-scale expression of the civilization's visual language; consistent with whatever symbolic system the eventual Story/Identity documents define.
- **Energy Veins** — the visible channels Energy Light travels through inside a material (most associated with Energy Crystal and Unknown Crystal).
- **Hidden Layers** — sub-surface detail only revealed under specific lighting or proximity conditions — the material equivalent of a Camera Reveal (Camera Bible §8): partial information now, full information only when the story earns it.

====================================================

## 6. REFLECTION PHILOSOPHY

Cross-referenced in full against Lighting Bible §10; this section defines the material-side parameters that lighting reads from.

**Gloss, Roughness, Mirror, Diffusion, Blur** are the primary dial-set every material family sets a baseline value for — Obsidian and Liquid Metal sit at the gloss/mirror end; White Stone and Architectural Stone sit at the roughness/diffusion end.

**Subsurface** is reserved for translucent families (Living Glass, Transparent Minerals, Energy Crystal) — light entering and softly scattering beneath the surface rather than bouncing cleanly off it.

**Refraction** is Unknown Crystal and Transparent Minerals' defining behavior — these families are understood primarily through what they do to light passing *through* them, not light bouncing *off* them.

No material family may claim more than two of these five properties as dominant — a family that is simultaneously highly mirrored, highly diffused, and highly subsurface has no clear optical identity and reads as inconsistent.

====================================================

## 7. MATERIAL EVOLUTION

Materials change through:

- **Time** — the aging language of §2, applied at the pace of the world's own history, not the visitor's single session.
- **Energy** — proximity to active Energy Light sources may visibly intensify Energy Veins (§5) in Energy Crystal and Unknown Crystal.
- **Presence** — Living Glass specifically is permitted to respond, subtly, to visitor proximity — the material equivalent of Lighting Bible §2's Living Light identity.
- **Distance** — some families (Ancient Alloy, Sacred Stone) reveal different levels of micro-detail (§5) at different camera distances, consistent with the Camera Bible's proximity-as-intimacy principle (Camera Bible §3).
- **Story** — a small number of hero materials are permitted authored, one-time state changes tied to specific narrative beats (never a generic "material gets better as story progresses" system).
- **Chapter** — a material family's baseline finish may shift slightly to match a chapter's dialect (cross-reference Lighting Bible §4) without changing its underlying identity (§2).
- **Environment** — the same material family may present with different dust/weathering/temperature-of-light response depending on which environment (§8) it appears in.

Material Evolution is always subtle. A material that visibly transforms in an obvious, showy way has crossed from "evolution" into "effect," and belongs, if anywhere, in the FX Bible instead.

====================================================

## 8. ENVIRONMENTAL MATERIAL DIALECTS

*Terminology note: these are Level 3 Environmental Dialects per the Canonical Naming Architecture (docs/bible/26) — reusable material palettes, never locations. See that document §3 for which Level 2 Physical Location uses which Dialect.*

Each Dialect (cross-reference Lighting Bible §8) has a dominant material palette, not an exclusive one:

- **The Reverent Dialect** *(formerly "Temple")* — Sacred Stone, Black Obsidian, Energy Crystal accents.
- **The Active Dialect** *(formerly "Workshop")* — Ancient Alloy, Liquid Metal, visible tool-wear micro-detail (§5).
- **The Hushed Dialect** *(formerly "Archive")* — Memory Stone, aged Transparent Minerals, the least reflective palette in the world.
- **The Attentive Dialect** *(formerly "Library")* — White Stone, small Unknown Crystal details, Ancient Alloy fixtures.
- **The Genesis Dialect** *(formerly "Origin Core" as an environment name)* — Energy Crystal and Sacred Stone at their most saturated and least aged — the "youngest-reading" surfaces in an otherwise ancient world.
- **The Vast Dialect** *(formerly "Observatory")* — White Stone and Transparent Minerals, chosen specifically for how little they compete visually with Lighting Bible §8's sky-dominant illumination.
- **The Generative Dialect** *(formerly "Creation Chamber")* — Living Glass and Energy Crystal dominant, the most evolution-active Dialect (§7) in the world.
- **The Recollective Dialect** *(formerly "Memory Hall")* — Memory Stone almost exclusively, the most restrained material palette in HEBRA, matching its restrained lighting Dialect (Lighting Bible §8).

====================================================

## 9. FORBIDDEN MATERIALS

Per Appendix D's standing authority:

- **Plastic** — no material in HEBRA reads as manufactured-synthetic in the contemporary, disposable sense.
- **Cheap Metal** — thin-reading, uniform, uninteresting metal finishes are forbidden; every metal family (§3) must justify its presence through history and behavior.
- **Generic Concrete** — architectural material with no traceable identity (§2) or family (§3) is forbidden outright.
- **Stock PBR** — any material recognizable as an unmodified marketplace or engine-default PBR preset is forbidden (cross-reference Appendix E's Extraction Method).
- **Marketplace Textures** — same standing prohibition as Appendix D's stock-asset section, applied specifically to material surfaces.
- **Artificial Perfection** — a material with zero imperfection (§5), zero aging (§2), and zero history (§4) does not belong in HEBRA regardless of how technically impressive it renders.

====================================================

## 10. PERFORMANCE PHILOSOPHY

Bound by Appendix F without exception.

**Texture Resolution** follows Appendix F's hero/standard/background/small-prop tiers directly — hero materials (§2, Material Hierarchy) receive the largest allocation; environmental and background materials are deliberately simpler.

**Compression** — KTX2/Basis Universal per Appendix F, applied without exception; no unnecessary PNG textures ship.

**Mipmaps** are mandatory for every material used at variable camera distance, which — given the Camera Bible's multi-axis navigation (Camera Bible §10) — is effectively every material in the world.

**Streaming** — material data loads progressively with the same atmosphere-first, detail-later priority Appendix F establishes for the world generally.

**Memory Budget** — tracked per material family against Appendix F's VRAM ceilings; hero families are budgeted first, environmental families fill the remainder.

**Material Instancing** — repeated environmental materials (§8) are instanced rather than duplicated, keeping draw calls within Appendix F's ceilings without visually flattening the material's authored micro-detail (§5), which is applied at the shader/detail-map level rather than through unique per-instance geometry.

====================================================

## 11. RESEARCH

Per Appendix E's standing protocol, applied here specifically:

**Industrial Design and Luxury Materials** — study how premium physical products communicate value through material choice and finish quality, informing §1's identity-through-surface principle.

**Architecture, Stone, Glass, Metal, Crystals** — studied directly per material family (§3) for plausible physical behavior (§4) even where HEBRA's materials are ultimately invented.

**Museum Materials** — study conservation and display practice for how aged, historic materials are documented and presented, directly informing §2's Material Aging language.

**PBR Theory** — studied for the physical honesty of light/material interaction, cross-referenced with Lighting Bible §13.

**Three.js, official documentation** — studied for technique only, never for ready-made material presets, per Appendix D.

Extract principles. Never imitate.

====================================================

## 12. MATERIAL OATH

I have a history before I have a texture.

I am never perfect. My imperfections are not noise — they are the record of something that happened to me.

I know what I am next to. My seams are designed, not accidental.

I behave the way my family behaves — my roughness, my gloss, my reflection are consistent with what kind of matter I am, not chosen for a single flattering shot.

I evolve slowly, and only when the story gives me a reason to.

I am never stock. I am never a marketplace default wearing a new color. I was made for HEBRA and I could not exist anywhere else.

I hold my performance budget without losing my identity — a cheaper version of me is still recognizably me.

Every future hand that authors a surface of HEBRA inherits this oath before it inherits any shader graph.

## 13. PRODUCTION CHECKLIST

- [ ] Does this material belong to one of the twelve families in §3, or has a new family been deliberately and fully documented per §4?
- [ ] Are all twelve required fields in §4 answered, not just the visually obvious ones?
- [ ] Does its micro-detail (§5) trace to plausible history rather than random noise?
- [ ] Does its reflection behavior (§6) match Lighting Bible §10's corresponding entry?
- [ ] Does its evolution, if any (§7), stay subtle enough to remain "material" rather than crossing into "effect"?
- [ ] Does it belong to its environment's palette (§8) or deliberately, legibly break from it?
- [ ] Has it been checked individually against every bullet in §9?
- [ ] Does it hold Appendix F's texture and memory budgets, measured per §10?

====================================================

## 14. APPROVAL CHECKLIST

- [ ] Identity: singular, describable in one sentence, distinguishable at a glance from every other family.
- [ ] History: written down before the material is finalized visually.
- [ ] Research: principle extracted and logged per §11, never a copied reference.
- [ ] Performance: reviewed against Appendix F on both desktop and mobile targets.
- [ ] Final Test (Appendix E): with all reference material hidden, does this surface still feel unmistakably HEBRA's?

If every box is checked, material work may proceed to Creative, Engineering, Performance, and Identity Review per the Production Roadmap. If any box is unchecked, the work returns to this document, not to the shader graph.
